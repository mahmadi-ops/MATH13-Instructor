Fact 3.3.10.

If \(\mathbf u\text{,}\) \(\mathbf v\text{,}\) and \(\mathbf w\) are any vectors and \(c\) is a scalar, then
🔗
  1. \(\displaystyle \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = \mathbf v \cdot \mathbf u\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle (c\mathbf u)\cdot \mathbf v = \mathbf u \cdot (c\mathbf v) = c(\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v)\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\displaystyle \mathbf u \cdot (\mathbf v + \mathbf w) = \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v + \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf w\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(\displaystyle \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf u = \|\mathbf u\|^2\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. \(\displaystyle \mathbf 0 \cdot \mathbf u = 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Write \(\mathbf u = \langle u_1,u_2,u_3\rangle\text{,}\) \(\mathbf v = \langle v_1,v_2,v_3\rangle\text{,}\) and \(\mathbf w = \langle w_1,w_2,w_3\rangle\text{.}\) Each property follows from (3.3.5) together with the corresponding property of real numbers.
🔗
  1. Multiplication of real numbers is commutative, so
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = u_1v_1+u_2v_2+u_3v_3 = v_1u_1+v_2u_2+v_3u_3 = \mathbf v \cdot \mathbf u. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Since \(c\mathbf u = \langle cu_1,cu_2,cu_3\rangle\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} (c\mathbf u)\cdot \mathbf v \amp = cu_1v_1+cu_2v_2+cu_3v_3 \\ \amp = c(u_1v_1+u_2v_2+u_3v_3) = c(\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v), \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    and the same computation applied to \(c\mathbf v = \langle cv_1,cv_2,cv_3\rangle\) gives \(\mathbf u \cdot (c\mathbf v) = c(\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Adding componentwise and then distributing,
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \mathbf u \cdot (\mathbf v + \mathbf w) \amp = u_1(v_1+w_1) + u_2(v_2+w_2) + u_3(v_3+w_3) \\ \amp = (u_1v_1+u_2v_2+u_3v_3) + (u_1w_1+u_2w_2+u_3w_3) \\ \amp = \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v + \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf w. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Taking \(\mathbf v = \mathbf u\) in (3.3.5), and recalling that \(\|\mathbf u\| = \sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2}\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u \cdot \mathbf u = u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2 = \left(\sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2}\right)^2 = \|\mathbf u\|^2. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Every component of \(\mathbf 0\) is zero, so \(\mathbf 0 \cdot \mathbf u = 0u_1+0u_2+0u_3 = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
