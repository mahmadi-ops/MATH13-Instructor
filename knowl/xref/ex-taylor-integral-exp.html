Example 2.3.14. A definite integral with no elementary antiderivative.

The function \(e^{-x^2}\) has no elementary antiderivative, so the integral
\begin{equation*} I = \int_0^1 e^{-x^2}\,dx \end{equation*}
cannot be evaluated with the fundamental theorem of calculus. Use the Taylor polynomial of order \(n\) of \(e^{u}\) to approximate \(I\text{,}\) bound the error with the Remainder Estimation Theorem, and find a value of \(n\) that guarantees an error of at most \(10^{-4}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
By Taylor’s formula for \(e^{u}\) at \(a = 0\) (see Subsection 2.3.2),
\begin{align*} e^{u} \amp = 1 + u + \frac{u^2}{2!} + \cdots + \frac{u^n}{n!} + R_n(u),\\ R_n(u) \amp = \frac{e^{c}}{(n+1)!}\,u^{n+1}, \end{align*}
for some \(c\) between \(0\) and \(u\text{.}\) Put \(u = -x^2\) with \(0 \le x \le 1\text{.}\) Then \(-1 \le u \le 0\text{,}\) so \(c \le 0\) and \(e^{c} \le 1\text{:}\) as in Remark 2.3.4 we may take \(M = 1\text{,}\) and
\begin{align*} e^{-x^2} \amp = 1 - x^2 + \frac{x^4}{2!} - \cdots\\ \amp \qquad + \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{n!} + R_n(-x^2),\\ \left|R_n(-x^2)\right| \amp \le \frac{x^{2n+2}}{(n+1)!}. \end{align*}
🔗
Now integrate from \(0\) to \(1\text{.}\) The polynomial part is a finite sum, so it can be integrated term by term:
\begin{align*} I \amp = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!}\int_0^1 x^{2k}\,dx + E_n\\ \amp = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!\,(2k+1)} + E_n, \end{align*}
where the error \(E_n = \int_0^1 R_n(-x^2)\,dx\) satisfies
\begin{equation} |E_n| \le \int_0^1 \frac{x^{2n+2}}{(n+1)!}\,dx = \frac{1}{(2n+3)\,(n+1)!}.\tag{2.3.8} \end{equation}
🔗
With \(n = 4\text{,}\)
\begin{align*} I \amp \approx 1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{42} + \frac{1}{216}\\ \amp \approx 0.74749,\\ |E_4| \amp \le \frac{1}{11 \cdot 5!} = \frac{1}{1320} \lt 0.0008. \end{align*}
For an error of at most \(10^{-4}\) we need \((2n+3)\,(n+1)! \ge 10^{4}\text{.}\) With \(n = 5\text{,}\) \(13 \cdot 720 = 9360\) falls just short; with \(n = 6\text{,}\) \(15 \cdot 5040 = 75600\text{.}\) So
\begin{equation*} I \approx \sum_{k=0}^{6} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!\,(2k+1)} \approx 0.74684 \end{equation*}
has error at most \(\frac{1}{75600} \lt 2 \cdot 10^{-5}\text{.}\) (The true value is \(I = 0.746824\ldots\text{.}\))
