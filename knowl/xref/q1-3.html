Worksheet Exercise 13.

A metal plate occupies the square \(0 \le x \le 5\text{,}\) \(0 \le y \le 5\text{,}\) and is heated so that the temperature at the point \((x,y)\) is \(T(x,y)\text{,}\) measured in degrees Fahrenheit. Distances are measured in feet. The table below records some measured values of \(T\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. Approximate \(T_x(1,1)\) and interpret your answer, with units, in the context of the problem.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Approximate \(T_{xy}(1,1)\text{,}\) and give its units.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. An ant walks across the plate along the path
    \begin{equation*} x = 1 - \frac{\ln(2t-3)}{8}, \qquad y = \frac{2^t}{4}, \qquad 1.5 \lt t \lt 4 . \end{equation*}
    Approximate the rate at which the temperature the ant feels is changing at the moment it is at the point \((x,y) = (1,1)\text{,}\) and give the units.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
(a) A partial derivative is a limit of difference quotients, so with the data we have we approximate it by a difference quotient. Holding \(y=1\) fixed and stepping forward in \(x\) from \(x=1\) to \(x=1.5\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} T_x(1,1) \approx \frac{T(1.5,1) - T(1,1)}{1.5-1} = \frac{74-70}{0.5} = 8 . \end{equation*}
The units are degrees Fahrenheit per foot. The interpretation: at the point \((1,1)\text{,}\) if the ant moves in the positive \(x\)-direction while its \(y\)-coordinate stays fixed, the temperature increases at a rate of about \(8\,{}^{\circ}\text{F}\) per foot.
🔗
(b) By definition \(T_{xy} = (T_x)_y\text{,}\) so we compute \(T_x\) at two different values of \(y\) and then take a difference quotient in \(y\text{.}\) We already have \(T_x(1,1) \approx 8\text{,}\) and in the same way
\begin{equation*} T_x(1,1.5) \approx \frac{T(1.5,1.5) - T(1,1.5)}{0.5} = \frac{68-65}{0.5} = 6 . \end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{equation*} T_{xy}(1,1) \approx \frac{T_x(1,1.5) - T_x(1,1)}{1.5-1} = \frac{6-8}{0.5} = -4 . \end{equation*}
The units are degrees Fahrenheit per foot per foot, i.e. \({}^{\circ}\text{F}/\text{ft}^2\text{.}\) This says that as we move in the \(y\)-direction, the rate at which the temperature rises in the \(x\)-direction is itself decreasing.
🔗
(c) First we find the value of \(t\) at which the ant is at \((1,1)\text{.}\) We need
\begin{align*} 1 - \frac{\ln(2t-3)}{8} = 1 \amp \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ln(2t-3) = 0\\ \amp \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2t-3 = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = 2 , \end{align*}
and indeed \(y = 2^2/4 = 1\) at \(t=2\text{,}\) as required. Note that \(t=2\) lies in the given interval \(1.5 \lt t \lt 4\text{.}\)
🔗
Next we differentiate the path:
\begin{align*} \frac{dx}{dt} \amp = -\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{2}{2t-3} = -\frac{1}{4(2t-3)}, \amp \frac{dx}{dt}\bigg|_{t=2} \amp = -\frac{1}{4} ,\\ \frac{dy}{dt} \amp = \frac{2^t \ln 2}{4}, \amp \frac{dy}{dt}\bigg|_{t=2} \amp = \frac{4\ln 2}{4} = \ln 2 . \end{align*}
🔗
We also need \(T_y(1,1)\text{,}\) which we approximate from the table just as before, stepping forward in \(y\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} T_y(1,1) \approx \frac{T(1,1.5) - T(1,1)}{0.5} = \frac{65-70}{0.5} = -10 . \end{equation*}
🔗
Now the chain rule gives
\begin{align*} \frac{dT}{dt} \amp = T_x\,\frac{dx}{dt} + T_y\,\frac{dy}{dt}\\ \amp \approx (8)\left(-\frac14\right) + (-10)(\ln 2)\\ \amp = -2 - 10\ln 2 \approx -8.93 . \end{align*}
The units are degrees Fahrenheit per unit of \(t\) (per minute, if \(t\) is measured in minutes). So the ant is cooling off at about \(8.9\,{}^{\circ}\text{F}\) per unit of time. This makes sense: the ant is moving slightly in the \(-x\) direction, which cools it a little, and it is moving in the \(+y\) direction, which cools it a lot. Figure 5.10.54 shows the situation.
