Example 2.3.11. A \(\frac{0}{0}\) limit by Taylor series.

Compute
\begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\arctan(x) - x}{\sin(x) - x}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Solution.
Both the numerator and the denominator tend to \(0\text{,}\) so the limit has the form \(\frac{0}{0}\text{.}\) L’Hôpital’s rule applies, but it would have to be used three times, and the derivatives of \(\arctan(x)\) get messy quickly. Taylor series give the answer in one line, because they show exactly how fast each side goes to zero.
🔗
Subtracting \(x\) from (2.3.6) and from (2.3.5),
\begin{align*} \arctan(x) - x \amp = -\frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{x^5}{5} - \cdots,\\ \sin(x) - x \amp = -\frac{x^3}{6} + \frac{x^5}{120} - \cdots. \end{align*}
Both differences vanish like \(x^3\text{,}\) and that is the information the limit needs. Factor \(x^3\) out of each,
\begin{align*} \arctan(x) - x \amp = x^3\left(-\frac{1}{3} + \frac{x^2}{5} - \cdots\right),\\ \sin(x) - x \amp = x^3\left(-\frac{1}{6} + \frac{x^2}{120} - \cdots\right), \end{align*}
and cancel it:
\begin{equation*} \frac{\arctan(x) - x}{\sin(x) - x} = \frac{-\frac{1}{3} + \frac{x^2}{5} - \cdots} {-\frac{1}{6} + \frac{x^2}{120} - \cdots}. \end{equation*}
As \(x \to 0\) every term after the first in each bracket tends to \(0\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\arctan(x) - x}{\sin(x) - x} = \frac{-1/3}{-1/6} = 2. \end{equation*}
🔗
Why may the dots be ignored? This is where the Remainder Theorem enters. Taylor’s formula (2.3.1) with \(a = 0\) and \(n = 4\) gives \(\sin(x) = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + R_4(x)\) with \(|R_4(x)| \le \frac{|x|^5}{5!}\text{,}\) because every derivative of \(\sin\) is bounded by \(1\text{;}\) hence \(\left|\frac{R_4(x)}{x^3}\right| \le \frac{x^2}{120} \to 0\text{.}\) Likewise \(\arctan(x) = x - \frac{x^3}{3} + \widetilde{R}_4(x)\) with \(|\widetilde{R}_4(x)| \le \frac{M}{5!}\,|x|^5\text{,}\) where \(M\) is a bound for the fifth derivative of \(\arctan\) on \([-1,1]\text{,}\) so \(\frac{\widetilde{R}_4(x)}{x^3} \to 0\) as well. The two brackets above are exactly \(-\frac{1}{3} + \frac{\widetilde{R}_4(x)}{x^3}\) and \(-\frac{1}{6} + \frac{R_4(x)}{x^3}\text{.}\)
