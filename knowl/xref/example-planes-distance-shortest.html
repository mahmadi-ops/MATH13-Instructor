Example 3.6.14. Shortest distance from a point to a plane.

Find the shortest distance from the point \(S(1,0,-2)\) to the plane \(x + 2y + z = 4\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
We need a point \(P\) on the plane. We choose \(x = y = 0\text{,}\) which when substituted into the equation of the plane \(x + 2y + z = 4\text{,}\) gives \(z = 4\text{.}\) Hence, we have a point \(P(0,0,4)\) on the plane. We then compute the vector \(\overrightarrow{PS} = \langle 1,0,-6\rangle\text{.}\) We also extract the normal vector \(\overrightarrow{n} = \langle 1,2,1\rangle\text{,}\) \(\|\overrightarrow{n}\| = \sqrt{6}\) from the equation of the plane, which is in the standard form (3.6.7).
\begin{equation*} \overrightarrow{PS}\cdot\overrightarrow{n} = 1 + 0 - 6 = -5 \end{equation*}
Substituting into (3.6.9) gives
\begin{equation*} d = \frac{\left|\overrightarrow{PS}\cdot\overrightarrow{n}\right|}{\|\overrightarrow{n}\|} = \frac{|-5|}{\sqrt{6}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{6}} = \frac{5}{6}\sqrt{6} \end{equation*}
🔗
The dot product came out negative because \(S\) lies on the side of the plane opposite to \(\overrightarrow{n}\text{,}\) as Figure 3.6.15 shows; the absolute value in (3.6.9) takes care of the sign.
🔗
We can also find the point \(Q\) of the plane closest to \(S\text{,}\) the foot of the perpendicular from \(S\text{.}\) Since \(\overrightarrow{PS} = \overrightarrow{PQ} + \overrightarrow{QS}\text{,}\) with \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\) in the plane and \(\overrightarrow{QS}\) along \(\overrightarrow{n}\text{,}\) we have \(\overrightarrow{QS} = \operatorname{proj}_{\overrightarrow{n}}\overrightarrow{PS}\text{,}\) so
\begin{align*} Q \amp= S - \frac{\overrightarrow{PS}\cdot\overrightarrow{n}}{\|\overrightarrow{n}\|^2}\,\overrightarrow{n} = (1,0,-2) + \frac{5}{6}\,\langle 1,2,1\rangle\\ \amp= \left(\frac{11}{6},\ \frac{5}{3},\ -\frac{7}{6}\right). \end{align*}
