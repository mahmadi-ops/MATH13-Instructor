Checkpoint 2.2.10.

Compute the Taylor series of \(f(x) = \dfrac{1}{3x+7}\) at \(x = 1\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Method 1: from the definition. The series is centred at \(x = 1\text{,}\) so what we need are the derivatives \(f^{(k)}(1)\text{.}\) Differentiating \(f(x) = (3x+7)^{-1}\) a few times, and leaving the factors of \(3\) that the chain rule produces on display rather than multiplying them out,
\begin{align*} f(x) \amp = (3x+7)^{-1},\\ f'(x) \amp = -3\,(3x+7)^{-2},\\ f''(x) \amp = 2!\,3^{2}(3x+7)^{-3},\\ f'''(x) \amp = -3!\,3^{3}(3x+7)^{-4}. \end{align*}
🔗
The pattern is now readable. Each differentiation flips the sign, brings down one more factor of \(3\) from the chain rule, raises the power in the denominator by one, and multiplies the factorial up by one. So for the \(k\)th derivative,
\begin{equation*} f^{(k)}(x) = (-1)^{k}\, k!\, 3^{k} (3x+7)^{-(k+1)}. \end{equation*}
🔗
At the centre, \(3(1) + 7 = 10\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} f^{(k)}(1) = \frac{(-1)^{k}\, k!\, 3^{k}}{10^{\,k+1}}, \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{f^{(k)}(1)}{k!} = \frac{(-1)^{k}\, 3^{k}}{10^{\,k+1}}. \end{equation*}
The \(k!\) from the derivative cancels the \(k!\) that Definition 2.2.1 divides by, which is what makes the coefficients so simple here. Substituting them in,
\begin{align*} f(x) \amp = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k}\, 3^{k}}{10^{\,k+1}} (x-1)^{k}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{3}{100}(x-1) + \frac{9}{1000}(x-1)^2 - \cdots \end{align*}
🔗
Method 2: as a geometric series. Rewrite the denominator in terms of \(x - 1\text{:}\) \(3x + 7 = 3(x-1) + 10\text{.}\) Factoring out the \(10\text{,}\)
\begin{align*} f(x) \amp = \frac{1}{10 + 3(x-1)} = \frac{1}{10} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{3(x-1)}{10}}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{10} \cdot \frac{1}{1 - \left(-\dfrac{3(x-1)}{10}\right)}. \end{align*}
🔗
The last expression is the sum of a geometric series with \(a = \dfrac{1}{10}\) and \(r = -\dfrac{3(x-1)}{10}\text{,}\) so by (2.1.7),
\begin{align*} f(x) \amp = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{10} \left(-\frac{3(x-1)}{10}\right)^{n-1}\\ \amp = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n}\,3^{n}}{10^{\,n+1}} (x-1)^{n}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{3}{100}(x-1) + \frac{9}{1000}(x-1)^2 - \cdots \end{align*}
🔗
This is the same series as Method 1, reached without differentiating anything. It also comes with its interval for free: a geometric series converges exactly when \(|r| \lt 1\text{,}\) which here says \(\left|\dfrac{3(x-1)}{10}\right| \lt 1\text{,}\) that is \(|x - 1| \lt \dfrac{10}{3}\text{.}\)
