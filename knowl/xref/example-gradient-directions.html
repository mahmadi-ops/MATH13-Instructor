Example 4.5.19. Example III.

Consider the function \(f(x,y) = \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{y^2}{2}\text{.}\) Find the directions in which, at the point \(P_0(1,1)\text{,}\)
🔗
  1. \(f\) increases most rapidly,
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(f\) decreases most rapidly,
    🔗
    🔗
  3. there is no change in \(f\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
A. As explained above, \(f\) increases most rapidly in the direction of \(\nabla f\text{.}\) We have
\begin{align} \nabla f \amp= x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)}\notag\\ \amp= \hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}.\tag{4.5.12} \end{align}
🔗
Note that we need to divide \(\left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)} = \hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}\) by its length to find the unit vector corresponding to the direction of most rapid increase:
\begin{equation} \frac{\left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)}}{\left\|\left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)}\right\|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\imath} + \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\jmath}.\tag{4.5.13} \end{equation}
🔗
B. For the direction of most rapid decrease, we take the opposite direction:
\begin{equation} -\frac{\left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)}}{\left\|\left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)}\right\|} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\imath} - \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\jmath}.\tag{4.5.14} \end{equation}
🔗
C. To find the directions of zero change in \(f\text{,}\) we require
\begin{align} \left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)}\cdot\hat{u} \amp= (\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})\cdot(u_1\hat{\imath} + u_2\hat{\jmath})\notag\\ \amp= u_1 + u_2\notag\\ \amp= 0 \quad\text{or}\quad u_1\notag\\ \amp= -u_2,\tag{4.5.15} \end{align}
and since \(\hat{u}\) is a unit vector, we have
\begin{equation} \|\hat{u}\|^2 = u_1^2 + u_2^2 = 1.\tag{4.5.16} \end{equation}
🔗
After substituting (4.5.15) in (4.5.16), we conclude
\begin{equation} 2u_1^2 = 1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad u_1 = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}.\tag{4.5.17} \end{equation}
🔗
Hence, the directions of zero change in \(f\) at \(P_0(1,1)\) are
\begin{align} \hat{u} \amp= \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\imath} - \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\jmath} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \tilde{\hat{u}}\notag\\ \amp= -\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\imath} + \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\jmath}.\tag{4.5.18} \end{align}
🔗
Note that \(\tilde{\hat{u}} = -\hat{u}\text{.}\) All four directions are shown in Figure 4.5.20: the directions of zero change are tangent to the level curve of \(f\) through \(P_0(1,1)\text{,}\) while the directions of most rapid increase and decrease are perpendicular to it.
