Example 2.2.4. Approximating \(e^x\) near \(x = 0\).

Write down the Taylor polynomials of orders \(1\text{,}\) \(2\text{,}\) and \(3\) generated by \(f(x) = e^x\) at \(x = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Every derivative of \(e^x\) is \(e^x\) again, so \(f(0) = f'(0) = f''(0) = f'''(0) = 1\text{,}\) and the coefficients \(f^{(k)}(0)/k!\) of Definition 2.2.2 are just \(1/k!\text{.}\) With \(a = 0\) this gives
\begin{align*} p_1(x) \amp = f(0) + f'(0)(x-0) = 1 + x,\\ p_2(x) \amp = 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2!},\\ p_3(x) \amp = 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2!} + \frac{x^3}{3!}. \end{align*}
🔗
The first of these is the tangent line to \(y = e^x\) at \(x = 0\text{.}\) As Figure 2.2.5 shows, each additional term keeps the polynomial close to the curve over a wider stretch.
