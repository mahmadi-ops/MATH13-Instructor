Example 2.1.13. A telescoping series with logarithms.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \ln\!\left(\frac{k+2}{k+1}\right)\text{,}\) if it converges and if it diverges, show that it does.
🔗
Solution.
We begin by writing the partial sum \(s_n\text{.}\)
\begin{align*} s_n \amp = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \ln\!\left(\frac{k+2}{k+1}\right) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \left(\ln(k+2) - \ln(k+1)\right)\\ \amp = \left(\ln 2 - \ln 1\right) + \left(\ln 3 - \ln 2\right) + \left(\ln 4 - \ln 3\right) + \cdots\\ \amp \quad + \left(\ln(n+1) - \ln(n)\right) + \left(\ln(n+2) - \ln(n+1)\right)\\ \amp = -\ln(1) + \ln(n+2) = \ln(n+2) \end{align*}
🔗
Note that \(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty} s_n = \lim\limits_{n\to\infty} \ln(n+2) = \infty\text{,}\) and hence the series diverges.
