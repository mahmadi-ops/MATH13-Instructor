Example 3.4.9. Computing a cross product using the first definition.

Use Definition 3.4.1 to compute
🔗
  1. \(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{\jmath}\text{,}\) and also \(\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath}\text{;}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\mathbf u \times \mathbf v\text{,}\) where \(\mathbf u = 4\hat{\imath}\) and \(\mathbf v\) is a vector of length \(3\) lying in the \(xy\)-plane, making an angle of \(30^\circ\) with \(\mathbf u\) measured counterclockwise.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
Part 1. The vectors \(\hat{\imath}\) and \(\hat{\jmath}\) are unit vectors, so \(\|\hat{\imath}\| = \|\hat{\jmath}\| = 1\text{,}\) and they are perpendicular, so \(\theta = 90^\circ\) and \(\sin\theta = 1\text{.}\) Both lie in the \(xy\)-plane, so the unit vector orthogonal to both is either \(\hat{k}\) or \(-\hat{k}\text{;}\) curling the fingers of the right hand from \(\hat{\imath}\) toward \(\hat{\jmath}\) points the thumb along \(+\hat{k}\) (Figure 3.4.10(a)), so \(\hat{n} = \hat{k}\) and
\begin{equation*} \hat{\imath} \times \hat{\jmath} = \left(\|\hat{\imath}\|\|\hat{\jmath}\|\sin 90^\circ\right)\hat{k} = (1)(1)(1)\,\hat{k} = \hat{k}. \end{equation*}
For \(\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath}\) the two lengths and the angle are unchanged, but the right-hand rule now runs from \(\hat{\jmath}\) toward \(\hat{\imath}\text{,}\) which points the thumb along \(-\hat{k}\text{.}\) Hence \(\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath} = -\hat{k} = -(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{\jmath})\text{,}\) in agreement with property 4 of Theorem 3.4.7.
🔗
Part 2. Here \(\|\mathbf u\| = 4\text{,}\) \(\|\mathbf v\| = 3\text{,}\) and \(\theta = 30^\circ\text{,}\) so the length of the cross product is
\begin{equation*} \|\mathbf u \times \mathbf v\| = \|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|\sin 30^\circ = (4)(3)\left(\tfrac12\right) = 6. \end{equation*}
Both vectors lie in the \(xy\)-plane, and turning from \(\mathbf u\) to \(\mathbf v\) is counterclockwise, so the right-hand rule again gives \(\hat{n} = \hat{k}\) and
\begin{equation*} \mathbf u \times \mathbf v = 6\,\hat{k} = \langle 0,0,6\rangle. \end{equation*}
🔗
As a check on the magnitude, Fact 3.4.3 says \(\|\mathbf u \times \mathbf v\|\) is the area of the parallelogram built on \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf v\text{:}\) its base is \(\|\mathbf u\| = 4\) and its height is \(\|\mathbf v\|\sin 30^\circ = 1.5\text{,}\) giving an area of \(6\) (Figure 3.4.10(b)).
