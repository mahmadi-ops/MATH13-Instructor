    Subsection 3.2.2 Vector Algebra Operations

    Definition 3.2.5. Vector Addition.

    Let \(\mathbf u = \langle u_1, u_2, u_3 \rangle\) and \(\mathbf v = \langle v_1, v_2, v_3 \rangle\text{.}\) The sum of \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf v\) is the vector
    \begin{equation} \mathbf u + \mathbf v = \langle u_1+v_1,\; u_2+v_2,\; u_3+v_3 \rangle.\tag{3.2.3} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗
    Parallelogram law. The parallelogram law tells us how to add two vectors geometrically, and is described in Figure 3.2.6. Placing the tail of \(\mathbf v\) at the head of \(\mathbf u\) produces the sum as the third side of a triangle; equivalently, drawing both vectors from a common initial point makes the sum the diagonal of the parallelogram they span.
    🔗

    Example 3.2.7. Adding Two Vectors.

    Add the following two vectors geometrically and algebraically.
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u = \langle 1,1 \rangle \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf v = \langle -2,1 \rangle \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    Algebraically, by (3.2.3) we have
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u + \mathbf v = \langle 1,1 \rangle + \langle -2,1 \rangle = \langle -1, 2 \rangle. \end{equation*}
    For the geometrical part we use the parallelogram law, as shown in Figure 3.2.8.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 3.2.9. Scalar Multiplication.

    Let \(\mathbf u = \langle u_1, u_2, u_3 \rangle\) and let \(k \in \R\) be a scalar. The scalar multiple \(k\,\mathbf u\) is the vector
    \begin{equation} k\,\mathbf u = \langle k u_1,\; k u_2,\; k u_3 \rangle.\tag{3.2.4} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗
    Note that both definitions can be reduced to the 2D case simply by removing the last components of the vectors.
    🔗
    Let \(k \in \R\) and \(\mathbf u = \langle u_1,u_2,u_3\rangle\text{.}\) Then the length of the vector \(k\mathbf u = \langle ku_1, ku_2, ku_3 \rangle\) is \(|k|\,\|\mathbf u\|\text{,}\) where \(|k|\) is the absolute value of \(k\) and \(\|\mathbf u\|\) is the length of \(\mathbf u\text{.}\) It is easy to see why this is the case:
    \begin{align} \|k\mathbf u\| \amp = \sqrt{k^2\left(u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2\right)}\notag\\ \amp = |k|\sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2+u_3^2} = |k|\,\|\mathbf u\|.\tag{3.2.5} \end{align}
    🔗
    The vector \(-\mathbf u\) has the same length as \(\mathbf u\text{;}\) however, the two vectors point in opposite directions. In general the length of \(k\mathbf u\) is \(|k|\) times the length of \(\mathbf u\text{,}\) and if \(k \gt 0\) the two vectors point in the same direction, whereas if \(k \lt 0\) they point in opposite directions. Figure 3.2.10 shows several examples.
    🔗

    Definition 3.2.11. Vector Subtraction.

    Subtraction is handled with the same idea: the difference \(\mathbf u - \mathbf v\) means
    \begin{equation} \mathbf u - \mathbf v = \mathbf u + (-\mathbf v),\tag{3.2.6} \end{equation}
    so we reverse \(\mathbf v\) and then add. Geometrically, \(\mathbf u - \mathbf v\) is the vector that points from the head of \(\mathbf v\) to the head of \(\mathbf u\) when both are drawn from a common initial point.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.2.12. Adding and Subtracting Geometrically.

    Given the two vectors \(\mathbf u = \langle 1,1 \rangle\) and \(\mathbf v = \langle -1,2 \rangle\text{,}\) find \(\mathbf u + \mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf u - \mathbf v\) geometrically.
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the sum we complete the parallelogram spanned by \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf v\text{.}\) For the difference we use (3.2.6): we first draw \(-\mathbf v = \langle 1,-2 \rangle\) and then add it to \(\mathbf u\text{,}\) which gives \(\mathbf u - \mathbf v = \langle 2,-1 \rangle\text{.}\) Both constructions are shown in Figure 3.2.13.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    The operations satisfy the familiar algebraic rules collected in Theorem 3.2.14.
    🔗

    Theorem 3.2.14. Properties of Vector Operations.

    Let \(\mathbf u\text{,}\) \(\mathbf v\text{,}\) \(\mathbf w\) be vectors and \(a\text{,}\) \(b\) be scalars. Then
    \begin{align*} 1.\;\; \mathbf u + \mathbf v \amp= \mathbf v + \mathbf u \amp\qquad 2.\;\; (\mathbf u + \mathbf v) + \mathbf w \amp= \mathbf u + (\mathbf v + \mathbf w)\\ 3.\;\; \mathbf u + \mathbf 0 \amp= \mathbf u \amp\qquad 4.\;\; \mathbf u + (-\mathbf u) \amp= \mathbf 0\\ 5.\;\; 0\,\mathbf u \amp= \mathbf 0 \amp\qquad 6.\;\; 1\,\mathbf u \amp= \mathbf u\\ 7.\;\; a(b\mathbf u) \amp= (ab)\mathbf u \amp\qquad 8.\;\; a(\mathbf u + \mathbf v) \amp= a\mathbf u + a\mathbf v\\ 9.\;\; (a+b)\mathbf u \amp= a\mathbf u + b\mathbf u \amp \amp \end{align*}
