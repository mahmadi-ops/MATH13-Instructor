    1. 3 points.

    Determine whether
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} \frac{2x^2y}{x^4+y^2} \end{equation*}
    exists or not. If it does, find the limit; if it does not, fully justify your answer.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Compare two paths into the origin. Along the \(y\)-axis, \(x = 0\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{y\to 0} \frac{0}{y^2} = \lim_{y\to 0} 0 = 0. \end{equation*}
    Along the parabola \(y = x^2\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{x\to 0} \frac{2x^2\cdot x^2}{x^4 + x^4} = \lim_{x\to 0} \frac{2x^4}{2x^4} = 1. \end{equation*}
    The two paths give different limits, \(0 \ne 1\text{,}\) so
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} \frac{2x^2y}{x^4+y^2} \quad\text{does not exist.} \end{equation*}

    2. 3 points.

    Which graph corresponds to \(2x^2 - y^2 + z^2 = 0\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Note: Fully justify your response. Correct answers with incorrect or unclear justifications will not earn points.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Look at the traces of \(2x^2 - y^2 + z^2 = 0\) in the three coordinate planes:
    \begin{align*} y = 0: \amp\quad 2x^2 + z^2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = z = 0,\\ z = 0: \amp\quad y^2 = 2x^2 \;\Rightarrow\; y = \pm\sqrt2\,x,\\ x = 0: \amp\quad z^2 = y^2 \;\Rightarrow\; z = \pm y. \end{align*}
    The first trace is only the origin; each of the other two is a pair of lines through the origin. The traces in the planes \(y = k\) are the ellipses \(2x^2 + z^2 = k^2\text{,}\) a single point for \(k = 0\) and growing with \(\lvert k\rvert\text{.}\) A surface that meets the plane \(y = 0\) only at the origin, is cut in pairs of lines through the origin by the other two coordinate planes, and has elliptical cross-sections growing linearly along the \(y\)-axis is a double cone with the \(y\)-axis as its axis:
    \begin{equation*} y^2 = 2x^2 + z^2. \end{equation*}
    This is Graph C.

    3. 4 points.

    Consider the function \(f(x,y) = \dfrac{x^2 - y^2}{x^2 + y^2}\text{.}\) Find all the directions \(\hat u\) such that \(\left(D_{\hat u} f\right)_{(1,1)} = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    By the quotient rule,
    \begin{align*} f_x \amp = \frac{2x(x^2+y^2) - 2x(x^2-y^2)}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\\ \amp = \frac{4xy^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2},\\ f_y \amp = \frac{-2y(x^2+y^2) - 2y(x^2-y^2)}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\\ \amp = \frac{-4x^2y}{(x^2+y^2)^2}, \end{align*}
    so \(f_x(1,1) = \frac44 = 1\text{,}\) \(f_y(1,1) = -\frac44 = -1\text{,}\) and \(\left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)} = \langle 1, -1\rangle\text{.}\) For a unit vector \(\hat u = \langle u_1, u_2\rangle\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} \left(D_{\hat u} f\right)_{(1,1)} \amp = \langle 1, -1\rangle\cdot\langle u_1, u_2\rangle\\ \amp = u_1 - u_2 = 0, \end{align*}
    so \(u_1 = u_2\text{.}\) Since \(\hat u\) is a unit vector, \(u_1^2 + u_2^2 = 1\text{,}\) so \(2u_1^2 = 1\) and \(u_1 = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\text{.}\) The two directions are
    \begin{align*} \hat u \amp = \left\langle \frac{1}{\sqrt2}, \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\right\rangle \qquad\text{and}\\ \hat u \amp = \left\langle -\frac{1}{\sqrt2}, -\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\right\rangle, \end{align*}
    the two unit vectors perpendicular to \(\left(\nabla f\right)_{(1,1)}\text{.}\)

    4. 3 points.

    The volume of a right circular cone is \(V = \frac13\pi r^2 h\text{,}\) where \(r\) is the radius of the base and \(h\) is the height of the cone. The radius is increasing at a rate of 3 inches per second and the height is decreasing at a rate of 5 inches per second. At what rate is the volume of the cone changing when the radius is 9 inches and the height is 9 inches? (Remember to include the units for your final answer.)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The data are \(\dfrac{dr}{dt} = 3\) and \(\dfrac{dh}{dt} = -5\text{,}\) at the instant when \(r = h = 9\text{.}\) The volume depends on \(t\) through \(r\) and \(h\text{,}\) so by the chain rule
    \begin{align*} \frac{dV}{dt} \amp = \frac{\partial V}{\partial r}\frac{dr}{dt} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial h}\frac{dh}{dt}\\ \amp = \left(\frac{2\pi}{3}rh\right)(3) + \left(\frac13\pi r^2\right)(-5). \end{align*}
    At \((r,h) = (9,9)\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \frac{dV}{dt}\bigg|_{(9,9)} \amp = \frac13\left[(6\pi)(9)(9) - (5\pi)(9)^2\right]\\ \amp = \frac{81\pi}{3} = 27\pi \ \ \frac{\text{in}^3}{\text{s}}. \end{align*}
    The volume is increasing at \(27\pi\) cubic inches per second.

    5. 2 points.

    Is there a function \(f(x,y)\text{,}\) with continuous second partial derivatives everywhere, such that
    \begin{align*} f_x(x,y) \amp = x + 2y \qquad\text{and}\\ f_y(x,y) \amp = 3x - y\ ? \end{align*}
    Fully justify your answer.
    🔗
    Solution.
    No. If there were such an \(f\text{,}\) its two mixed partial derivatives would be
    \begin{align*} f_{xy} \amp = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\bigl(f_x\bigr) = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x + 2y) = 2,\\ f_{yx} \amp = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl(f_y\bigr) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(3x - y) = 3 . \end{align*}
    But \(f\) and all its partial derivatives up to order two are continuous, so by the Mixed Derivative Theorem, Theorem 4.3.14, \(f_{xy} = f_{yx}\) at every point. Since \(2 \ne 3\text{,}\) no such function exists.
    🔗

    6. 2 points.

    Mark the following statements as True or False. If a statement is true, prove it, and if a statement is false, provide a counterexample.
    🔗

    (a)

    Assume that we evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} f(x,y)\) along the lines \(y = mx\) (\(m \ne 0\)). If the result of \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} f(x,y)\) is independent of \(m\text{,}\) then the limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} f(x,y)\) exists at \((0,0)\text{.}\) (1 point)
    🔗
    Solution.
    False. A counterexample is \(f(x,y) = \dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y}\text{.}\) Along a line \(y = mx\) with \(m \ne 0\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} \lim_{x\to 0} \frac{x^2}{x^2 + mx} \amp = \lim_{x\to 0} \frac{x^2}{x(x+m)}\\ \amp = \lim_{x\to 0} \frac{x}{x+m} = 0, \end{align*}
    the same for every \(m \ne 0\text{.}\) But along the line \(y = 0\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{x\to 0} \frac{x^2}{x^2} = 1 \ne 0, \end{equation*}
    so \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} \frac{x^2}{x^2+y}\) does not exist.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    If \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(a,b)} f(x,y)\) exists along any path that goes through \((a,b)\text{,}\) then the function \(f(x,y)\) is continuous at the point \((a,b)\text{.}\) (1 point)
    🔗
    Solution.
    False. Take \(f(x,y) = \dfrac{x^2 - y^2}{x + y}\) at \((a,b) = (0,0)\text{.}\) Wherever \(f\) is defined, \(x + y \ne 0\) and \(f(x,y) = x - y\text{,}\) so along every path
    \begin{align*} \lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} \frac{x^2 - y^2}{x + y} \amp = \lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)} (x - y)\\ \amp = 0 . \end{align*}
    However, \(f\) is not defined at \((0,0)\text{,}\) so it is not continuous there: continuity needs the limit to exist and to equal the value of the function at the point.
    🔗

    7. 3 points.

    Consider the ellipsoid \(x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 = 21\) and the point \(P_0(1,-2,2)\) on it.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find an equation of the tangent plane to the ellipsoid at \(P_0\text{.}\) (2 points)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First check that \(P_0\) is on the surface: \(1 + 2(4) + 3(4) = 21\text{.}\) The ellipsoid is the level surface \(F(x,y,z) = 21\) of \(F(x,y,z) = x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2\text{,}\) and the gradient of \(F\) is normal to its level surfaces:
    \begin{align*} \nabla F \amp = \langle 2x,\ 4y,\ 6z\rangle,\\ \nabla F\big|_{P_0} \amp = \langle 2,\ -8,\ 12\rangle . \end{align*}
    Any nonzero multiple is also normal, so take \(\vec n = \frac12\nabla F\big|_{P_0} = \langle 1, -4, 6\rangle\text{.}\) The tangent plane through \(P_0\) with normal \(\vec n\) is
    \begin{align*} \amp (x - 1) - 4(y + 2) + 6(z - 2) = 0,\\ \amp \text{that is,}\quad x - 4y + 6z = 21 . \end{align*}
    Check: \(P_0\) satisfies it, since \(1 + 8 + 12 = 21\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find parametric equations of the line normal to the ellipsoid at \(P_0\text{.}\) (1 point)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The normal line passes through \(P_0(1,-2,2)\) in the direction of \(\vec n = \langle 1, -4, 6\rangle\text{:}\)
    \begin{gather*} x = 1 + t, \qquad y = -2 - 4t,\\ z = 2 + 6t, \qquad t \in \mathbb{R}. \end{gather*}
