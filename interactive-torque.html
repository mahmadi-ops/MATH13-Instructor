Instructions.
In the left pane the axis of rotation passes through the marked point, perpendicular to the screen. Drag the head of the yellow position vector \(\overrightarrow{r}\) to move the point where the force is applied, and drag the head of the red force \(\overrightarrow{F}\) to change its size and direction. The dashed red line is the line along which the force acts, and the violet segment is the lever arm \(r_\perp = \|\overrightarrow{r}\|\sin\theta\text{,}\) the perpendicular distance from the axis to that line. The shaded parallelogram built on \(\overrightarrow{r}\) and \(\overrightarrow{F}\) has area \(\|\overrightarrow{\tau}\|\text{,}\) and the curved arrow shows the sense of the rotation the torque produces.
🔗
The right pane shows the same two vectors in space, together with \(\overrightarrow{\tau} = \overrightarrow{r} \times \overrightarrow{F}\) along the axis; drag that pane to view it from another angle, or press “view” to restore the original one. Press “animate” to swing \(\overrightarrow{F}\) once around the point of application: watch \(\|\overrightarrow{\tau}\|\) reach its largest value when \(\overrightarrow{F} \perp \overrightarrow{r}\text{,}\) fall to zero when \(\overrightarrow{F}\) points straight at (or away from) the axis, and reverse direction as the rotation changes sense. Either vector can also be moved with the keyboard (press “Tab” to reach its head, then use the arrow keys).
