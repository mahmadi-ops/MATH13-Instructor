    Subsection 4.4.4 A Doppler-Effect Activity

    Activity 4.4.4.1. Meeting Waves from a Moving Boat.

    When a boat travels against approaching waves, it meets crests more frequently. When it travels with the waves, it meets them less frequently. This activity uses the chain rule to explain both observations.
    🔗
    Three quantities must be kept distinct. The function \(x_b(t)\) is the boat’s horizontal position, \(x_c(t)\) is the horizontal position of one selected wave crest, and \(w(t)\) is the signed vertical water height at the boat, measured from the mean water level. Thus \(x_b(t)\) and \(x_c(t)\) are coordinates on the same position axis, while \(w(t)\) points perpendicular to that axis.
    🔗
    Consider equally spaced water waves traveling to the right along a straight line. Model the water-surface height at the boat by
    \begin{align} w\amp=f(x_b,x_c)\tag{4.4.8}\\ \amp=A\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(x_b-x_c)\right).\tag{4.4.9} \end{align}
    Here \(A>0\) is the wave amplitude and \(\lambda>0\) is the distance between neighboring crests. The model treats the waves as ideal sinusoids and neglects the boat’s effect on the water.
    🔗
    Suppose the boat travels with constant velocity \(v\text{,}\) while every crest travels to the right with constant speed \(k>0\text{:}\)
    \begin{align} x_b(t)\amp=vt,\tag{4.4.10}\\ x_c(t)\amp=kt.\tag{4.4.11} \end{align}
    At \(t=0\text{,}\) the boat and the selected crest are both at the origin. A positive \(v\) means the boat travels with the waves, and a negative \(v\) means it travels against them.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the rate of change of \(w\) with respect to \(t\) using the chain rule.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let \(\theta=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(x_b-x_c)\text{.}\) The two partial derivatives are
    \begin{align*} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_b} \amp=-\frac{2\pi A}{\lambda}\sin\theta,\\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_c} \amp=\frac{2\pi A}{\lambda}\sin\theta. \end{align*}
    Evaluating at \((x_b(t),x_c(t))\) and following the two paths in Figure 4.4.24 gives
    \begin{align} \frac{dw}{dt} \amp=f_{x_b}x_b'+f_{x_c}x_c'\tag{4.4.12}\\ \amp=\frac{2\pi A}{\lambda}\sin\theta\,(-v+k)\tag{4.4.13}\\ \amp=\frac{2\pi A}{\lambda}(k-v)\sin\theta.\tag{4.4.14} \end{align}
    Using \(x_b-x_c=(v-k)t\text{,}\) define \(\theta(t)=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(v-k)t\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dw}{dt}=\frac{2\pi A}{\lambda}(k-v)\sin\theta(t). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How can the water-surface height at the boat stay at zero (the mean water level) for all time, even though the waves are moving?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The boat must keep pace with the waves. If \(v\ne k\text{,}\) the distance \(x_b-x_c\) keeps changing, so the height at the boat runs through crests and troughs. If \(v=k\text{,}\) then \(x_b-x_c\) is constant, so the height is constant, and it is zero exactly when the boat sits a quarter wavelength from a crest, where the cosine in the model vanishes. For example, choose
    \begin{align*} x_b(t)\amp=kt-\frac{\lambda}{4},\\ x_c(t)\amp=kt. \end{align*}
    The phase is \(-\pi/2\text{,}\) so \(w(t)=A\cos(-\pi/2)=0\) for all \(t\text{,}\) and since \(\sin\theta=-1\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_b}\frac{dx_b}{dt} \amp=\frac{2\pi Ak}{\lambda},\\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_c}\frac{dx_c}{dt} \amp=-\frac{2\pi Ak}{\lambda}. \end{align*}
    🔗
    The equal, nonzero terms cancel, so \(dw/dt=0\text{.}\) A zero derivative at one isolated crest or trough need not mean \(v=k\text{;}\) matching speeds is what keeps the height constant over time. Any other fixed offset holds some other constant height between \(-A\) and \(A\text{.}\) At a tracked crest, for instance, \(w(t)=A\text{,}\) but there both chain-rule terms are \(0\) and nothing cancels.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    How does the boat’s velocity affect the time between successive crests?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Substituting the position functions into the model gives
    \begin{equation*} w(t)=A\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(v-k)t\right). \end{equation*}
    For \(v\ne k\text{,}\) one complete cycle occurs when the cosine’s argument changes by \(2\pi\text{.}\) Therefore the time between successive crests is
    \begin{equation} T=\frac{\lambda}{|k-v|}, \qquad v\ne k.\tag{4.4.15} \end{equation}
    Equivalently, the encounter frequency is \(|k-v|/\lambda\text{.}\) When \(v=k\text{,}\) the sampled height is constant, so it has no finite fundamental period.
    🔗
    For example, suppose adjacent crests are \(8\) meters apart and travel at \(2\) meters per second.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Can the boat measure the wave speed and wavelength? Suppose \(k\) and \(\lambda\) are unknown. A stationary boat encounters crests every \(4\) seconds. Traveling with the waves at \(1\text{ m/s}\text{,}\) it encounters them every \(8\) seconds. Assuming the crests move faster than the boat, determine \(k\) and \(\lambda\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using meters and seconds, the period formula gives
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 4\amp=\frac{\lambda}{k},\\ 8\amp=\frac{\lambda}{k-1}. \end{align*}
    Here \(k-1>0\) by assumption. Thus
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \lambda\amp=4k=8(k-1),\\ k\amp=2,\qquad \lambda=8. \end{align*}
    Therefore the wave speed is \(2\text{ m/s}\) and the wavelength is \(8\text{ m}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    What if the waves grow taller while the boat keeps pace with a crest? Let \(A=A(t)\text{,}\) with \(\lambda\) fixed. Must the measured height remain constant? What additional chain-rule term is needed?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Regard the height as the three-variable function
    🔗
    \begin{align*} w\amp=f(A,x_b,x_c)\\ \amp=A\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(x_b-x_c)\right). \end{align*}
    The chain rule now includes the amplitude path:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{dw}{dt} \amp=\frac{\partial f}{\partial A}\frac{dA}{dt}\\ \amp\quad+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_b}\frac{dx_b}{dt}\\ \amp\quad+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_c}\frac{dx_c}{dt}. \end{align*}
    Writing \(\phi=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(x_b-x_c)\text{,}\) this becomes
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{dw}{dt}\amp=A'(t)\cos\phi\\ \amp\quad+\frac{2\pi A(t)}{\lambda}(k-v)\sin\phi. \end{align*}
    At a tracked crest, \(\phi\) is a multiple of \(2\pi\) and \(v=k\text{.}\) Hence
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dw}{dt}=A'(t). \end{equation*}
    The measured height is therefore constant only when \(A'(t)=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    What if both the relative position and amplitude are changing? Let \(\lambda=8\text{ m}\) and, for \(t\ge 0\text{,}\) let
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A(t)\amp=(1\text{ m/s})t,\\ x_b(t)\amp=(1\text{ m/s})t,\\ x_c(t)\amp=(2\text{ m/s})t. \end{align*}
    Thus \(v=1\text{ m/s}\) and \(k=2\text{ m/s}\text{.}\) At \(t=1\text{ s}\text{,}\) find \(dw/dt\text{.}\) Identify the contribution from the changing amplitude, then express the position contribution both as one relative-position term and as separate boat and crest terms.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Set \(r=x_b-x_c\) and \(\phi=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}r\text{.}\) The three-variable chain rule gives
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{dw}{dt} \amp=f_AA'\\ \amp\quad+f_{x_b}x_b'\\ \amp\quad+f_{x_c}x_c'. \end{align*}
    Combining only the two position paths, write
    🔗
    \begin{align*} C_r \amp=f_{x_b}x_b'+f_{x_c}x_c'\\ \amp=-\frac{2\pi A}{\lambda}r'\sin\phi. \end{align*}
    Therefore
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \frac{dw}{dt} \amp=A'\cos\phi+C_r\\ \amp=A'\cos\phi -\frac{2\pi A}{\lambda}r'\sin\phi. \end{align*}
    At \(t=1\text{ s}\text{,}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A(1)\amp=1\text{ m}, \qquad A'(1)=1\text{ m/s},\\ r(1)\amp=-1\text{ m}, \qquad r'(1)=-1\text{ m/s},\\ \phi(1)\amp=-\frac{\pi}{4}. \end{align*}
    The amplitude contribution is
    🔗
    \begin{align*} C_A \amp=A'(1)\cos\phi(1)\\ \amp=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\text{ m/s}\\ \amp\approx0.707\text{ m/s}. \end{align*}
    The combined relative-position contribution is
    🔗
    \begin{align*} C_r \amp=-\frac{2\pi A(1)}{\lambda} r'(1)\sin\phi(1)\\ \amp=-\frac{\pi\sqrt{2}}{8}\text{ m/s}\\ \amp\approx-0.555\text{ m/s}. \end{align*}
    Equivalently, keeping the boat and crest paths separate gives
    🔗
    \begin{align*} f_{x_b}v \amp=\frac{\pi\sqrt{2}}{8}\text{ m/s},\\ f_{x_c}k \amp=-\frac{\pi\sqrt{2}}{4}\text{ m/s}. \end{align*}
    These two terms have sum
    🔗
    \begin{align*} f_{x_b}v+f_{x_c}k \amp=-\frac{\pi\sqrt{2}}{8}\text{ m/s}\\ \amp=C_r, \end{align*}
    which agrees with the combined relative-position calculation. Finally,
    🔗
    \begin{align*} w'(1) \amp=C_A+C_r\\ \amp=\frac{\sqrt{2}(4-\pi)}{8}\text{ m/s}\\ \amp\approx0.152\text{ m/s}. \end{align*}
    The positive net rate means that the growing amplitude outweighs the decrease caused by the boat moving away from the selected crest at this instant.
