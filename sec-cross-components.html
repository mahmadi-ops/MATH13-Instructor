    Subsection 3.4.2 Second Definition of the Cross Product

    Writing the vectors in components gives another definition for the cross product.
    🔗

    Definition 3.4.10. The Cross Product (Second Definition).

    If \(\mathbf u = \langle u_1, u_2, u_3\rangle\) and \(\mathbf v = \langle v_1, v_2, v_3\rangle\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation} \mathbf u \times \mathbf v = \langle u_2v_3 - u_3v_2,\; u_3v_1 - u_1v_3,\; u_1v_2 - u_2v_1\rangle.\tag{3.4.4} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗
    One way to remember the second definition of the cross product is to write it as a determinant:
    \begin{align} \mathbf u \times \mathbf v \amp = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\imath} \amp \hat{\jmath} \amp \hat{k} \\ u_1 \amp u_2 \amp u_3 \\ v_1 \amp v_2 \amp v_3 \end{vmatrix}\notag\\ \amp = \begin{vmatrix} u_2 \amp u_3 \\ v_2 \amp v_3 \end{vmatrix}\hat{\imath} - \begin{vmatrix} u_1 \amp u_3 \\ v_1 \amp v_3 \end{vmatrix}\hat{\jmath} + \begin{vmatrix} u_1 \amp u_2 \\ v_1 \amp v_2 \end{vmatrix}\hat{k},\tag{3.4.5} \end{align}
    where
    \begin{equation} \begin{vmatrix} a \amp b \\ c \amp d \end{vmatrix} = ad - bc.\tag{3.4.6} \end{equation}
    🔗

    Example 3.4.11. Computing a cross product.

    Calculate the cross product \(\mathbf u \times \mathbf v\) if \(\mathbf u = \langle 1,-1,2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf v = \langle 3,-2,1\rangle\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    By (3.4.5) and (3.4.6),
    \begin{align*} \mathbf u \times \mathbf v \amp= \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\imath} \amp \hat{\jmath} \amp \hat{k} \\ 1 \amp -1 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp -2 \amp 1 \end{vmatrix}\\ \amp= \begin{vmatrix} -1 \amp 2 \\ -2 \amp 1 \end{vmatrix}\hat{\imath} - \begin{vmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp 1 \end{vmatrix}\hat{\jmath} + \begin{vmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \\ 3 \amp -2 \end{vmatrix}\hat{k}\\ \amp= (-1+4)\hat{\imath} - (1-6)\hat{\jmath} + (-2+3)\hat{k} = 3\hat{\imath} + 5\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k} = \langle 3,5,1\rangle. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.4.12. Computing a vector perpendicular to two other vectors.

    Consider the points \(A(1,-1,2)\text{,}\) \(B(2,0,-1)\text{,}\) and \(C(0,-2,3)\text{.}\) Find a unit vector which is orthogonal to both \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We first compute the two vectors:
    \begin{align*} \overrightarrow{AB} \amp= \langle 1,1,-3\rangle\\ \overrightarrow{AC} \amp= \langle -1,-1,1\rangle. \end{align*}
    🔗
    The cross product \(\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\) is orthogonal to both vectors \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC}\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} \amp= \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\imath} \amp \hat{\jmath} \amp \hat{k} \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp -3 \\ -1 \amp -1 \amp 1 \end{vmatrix}\\ \amp= \begin{vmatrix} 1 \amp -3 \\ -1 \amp 1 \end{vmatrix}\hat{\imath} - \begin{vmatrix} 1 \amp -3 \\ -1 \amp 1 \end{vmatrix}\hat{\jmath} + \begin{vmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp -1 \end{vmatrix}\hat{k}\\ \amp= (1-3)\hat{\imath} - (1-3)\hat{\jmath} + (-1+1)\hat{k} = -2\hat{\imath} + 2\hat{\jmath} = \langle -2,2,0\rangle. \end{align*}
    🔗
    The unit vector in question is then computed as follows:
    \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \hat{n} \amp = \frac{\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}}{\left\|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\right\|}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{\sqrt 8}\langle -2,2,0\rangle = \left\langle -\frac{1}{\sqrt 2},\frac{1}{\sqrt 2},0\right\rangle = -\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\imath} + \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\hat{\jmath}. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.4.14. Computing the area of a triangle.

    Find the area of the triangle with the vertices \(A(1,-1,2)\text{,}\) \(B(2,0,-1)\text{,}\) and \(C(0,-2,3)\text{.}\) Note that these are the same points that we had in the previous example.
    🔗
    Solution.
    To find the area of the triangle, we first compute the area of the parallelogram built on \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC}\text{,}\) shown in Figure 3.4.15, and then divide by \(2\text{.}\) By (3.4.2),
    \begin{align*} \text{Area of the parallelogram} \amp= \left\|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\right\| = \sqrt 8 = 2\sqrt 2\\ \text{Area of the triangle} \amp= \frac12 \left\|\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}\right\| = \sqrt 2. \end{align*}
